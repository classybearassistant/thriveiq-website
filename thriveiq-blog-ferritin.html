Ferritin: The Iron Marker Your Doctor Probably Didn't Check

Educational content only. Not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about what's right for you.

Here's something that genuinely surprised me when I learned it: iron and ferritin are not the same thing — and most standard blood panels only check one of them.

If you've ever been told your iron is fine while feeling exhausted, losing hair, and struggling to think clearly — this might be the missing piece.

The bank account analogy that finally made it click

Iron is what your blood carries right now. Ferritin is where your body stores it.

Think of it like a bank account. Your serum iron is the cash in your wallet — what's available today. Ferritin is your savings account — what your body has in reserve.

You can have enough in your wallet and still be broke. Same principle.

When ferritin gets low, your body starts rationing. It keeps essential functions running and cuts what it considers optional — and the first things to go are hair growth, sustained energy, and clear thinking.

The symptoms nobody connects to this

What gets me is how often these symptoms get blamed on stress, aging, or just being a woman:

  • Chronic fatigue that doesn't improve with sleep
  • Hair loss or thinning — especially in women
  • Brain fog and difficulty concentrating
  • Feeling cold all the time
  • Poor exercise recovery
  • Restless legs at night
  • Shortness of breath with minimal exertion

Low ferritin is one of the most commonly missed causes of all of these — and it frequently gets overlooked because it requires a separate test that has to be ordered specifically.

Why "your iron is fine" isn't the whole answer

A standard CBC checks hemoglobin and hematocrit — the markers that flag anemia. You won't be flagged as anemic until things are quite depleted.

Even when ferritin IS checked, the conventional normal range bottoms out around 12–15 ng/mL. That's the level at which your body is in crisis mode — not the level at which you actually feel well.

Functional medicine practitioners typically look for ferritin above 50 ng/mL for women to feel their best. A ferritin of 14 shows up as "normal" on your lab report. For most women, it doesn't feel normal at all.

What the numbers mean

Below 12 ng/mLDepleted, clinical concern
12–30 ng/mLLow, commonly associated with fatigue and hair loss
30–50 ng/mLSuboptimal, worth a conversation if you have symptoms
50–100 ng/mLFunctional optimal range for most women
Above 150 ng/mLWorth discussing with your doctor as elevated ferritin has its own implications

These are reference ranges, not diagnoses. Always discuss your specific numbers with your healthcare provider.

Who tends to run low

Low ferritin is most common in women with heavy periods, vegetarians and vegans, anyone with gut absorption issues, distance runners, and women who are pregnant or recently postpartum. If any of that sounds familiar and you have unexplained fatigue or hair loss, ferritin is worth asking about specifically.

What I'd ask at my next appointment

  • Can we check my ferritin specifically — not just my CBC?
  • What is my ferritin number, not just whether it's in range?
  • If it's below 50, what are our options?

Your iron being "fine" is only half the story. Ferritin is the other half — and it's the half that explains how you actually feel.
