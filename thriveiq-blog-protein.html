Why Women Need More Protein Than They Think

Educational content only. Not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about what's right for you.

This one genuinely changed how I eat. Not in a complicated way — just in a "why didn't anyone tell me this sooner" way.

The standard protein recommendation most of us grew up hearing was calculated to prevent deficiency — not to support muscle maintenance, healthy aging, or metabolic health. For most women, especially over 35, it isn't enough.

What protein actually does

Protein isn't just for people who work out. It's the raw material for nearly everything your body builds and runs on:

  • Muscle tissue — building it and maintaining it
  • Hormones — including insulin, thyroid hormones, and estrogen metabolites
  • Neurotransmitters — serotonin, dopamine
  • Enzymes — every biochemical reaction requires them
  • Immune function — antibodies are proteins
  • Hair, skin, and nails — collagen and keratin are proteins

When protein intake is chronically low, your body prioritizes. The essential functions get what they need. Hair, skin, nails, and muscle maintenance get what's left over.

Why it matters more after 35

Starting in your mid-30s, muscle mass begins to decline at roughly 1% per year — a process called sarcopenia. After menopause, the rate accelerates.

Muscle is metabolically active. It burns calories at rest, improves insulin sensitivity, supports bone density, and protects joints. Losing it quietly over years is one of the primary drivers of the metabolic shifts women notice in their 40s and 50s — the slower metabolism, the weight that won't move, the fatigue.

The most effective way to slow that process is adequate protein combined with resistance exercise. Protein provides the building blocks. Movement provides the signal to use them.

How much is actually enough

Most research on active women and healthy aging points to 1.2–1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight — significantly higher than the standard 0.8g recommendation.

For a 140-pound woman, that's roughly 75–100 grams per day. Most women eating a typical diet get 50–60 grams. The gap is real.

What hitting the target actually looks like in a day:

Breakfast — 3 eggs + Greek yogurt~30–35g
Lunch — 4oz chicken or salmon + legumes~35–40g
Dinner — 4–5oz quality protein~30–35g
Total~95–110g

This isn't a meal plan. It's just an illustration of how intentional you have to be — especially at breakfast and lunch.

Signs worth paying attention to

  • Hair loss or thinning
  • Brittle nails
  • Slow wound healing
  • Fatigue that doesn't improve with sleep
  • Difficulty building or maintaining muscle despite exercise
  • Feeling hungry shortly after meals

What I'd ask at my next appointment

  • Is my protein intake likely adequate given my age and activity level?
  • Could low protein be contributing to my fatigue or hair loss?
  • Is resistance exercise something worth prioritizing alongside nutrition?

Protein isn't a trend. It's a building block your body runs on — and most women aren't getting enough of it.
