Why Your Cholesterol Isn't the Problem — The Fire Is

Educational content only. Not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about what's right for you.

Your doctor called. Your LDL is high. They want to put you on a statin.

Before you fill that prescription, ask yourself one question: is your doctor treating the fire, or the smoke alarm?

Here's what nobody explains in a 10-minute appointment.

When your arteries get damaged — from inflammation, seed oils, sugar, stress — your body sends cholesterol to the scene to patch the damage. Cholesterol isn't causing the problem. It's responding to one.

Think of it this way. Imagine firefighters showing up at a burning building. If you drove past and saw the trucks, you wouldn't say "those firefighters are destroying the neighborhood." You'd know they were called there because something was already wrong.

That's cholesterol. It's the fire truck, not the fire.

The real question your doctor should be asking isn't "how do we lower your LDL?" It's "why is your body sending so many fire trucks?"

The answers are almost always in your food, your inflammation markers, and your metabolic health — not in a pill.

What to ask at your next appointment

  • Can we check my hs-CRP to measure inflammation?
  • Can we run a fasting insulin — not just fasting glucose?
  • Can we look at my Triglyceride/HDL ratio instead of just total cholesterol?

Your cholesterol number is a signal. The question worth asking is what it's actually signaling.
